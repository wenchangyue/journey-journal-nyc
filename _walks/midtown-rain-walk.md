## The journey

The walk opens on **East 34th Street** in light rain, near The Long Hall
pub between Park and Madison, and heads west: across **Fifth Avenue** in
the shadow of the **Empire State Building's** block, past **Herald
Square** and **Macy's** — the 1902 flagship that remains the largest
department store in the United States — to the **New Yorker Hotel** at
Eighth Avenue, the 1930 Art Deco tower where Nikola Tesla lived out his
last decade in room 3327.

After a break, the rain has truly arrived. The second act starts in the
**Garment District**, where most of America's clothing was once sewn
within a few blocks, and follows the **Broadway pedestrian plazas** —
car-free since 2009 — north into **Times Square**. This is the walk's
heart: 200,000-people-a-day pavement thinned to umbrellas, billboards
doubled in the wet street, steam rising through the rain, and the
supertall towers above losing their tops in the mist.

At 48th Street the route turns from the neon up **Seventh Avenue**, then
cuts east through the quieter **East 50s** — scaffolding, string-lit
trees, luxury storefronts — past the Gothic bulk of **St. Patrick's
Cathedral**, which fills the entire block from 50th to 51st Street, and
ends on **East 51st** as the rain keeps falling.

Original city sound throughout — no narration, no music. The whole video
plays at a calm **0.9× speed** with the audio time-stretched and its
pitch preserved: every horn, voice and rain-hiss sounds exactly as it did
on the street, just a touch slower.
