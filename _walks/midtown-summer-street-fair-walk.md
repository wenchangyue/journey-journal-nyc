  - q: What are the Villard Houses?

      Avenue and 50th–51st Streets, composed as a single Italian Renaissance
      palazzo. A NYC Landmark since 1968, they now form the entrance of the
      Lotte New York Palace hotel.

## The journey

The walk begins in the shade of the **Park Avenue Viaduct** at **Pershing
Square**, where taxis curl around Grand Central Terminal beneath arches built
between 1919 and 1928. Past the **Graybar Building** at 420 Lexington Avenue,
the avenue suddenly changes character: a **summer street festival** has closed
Lexington to traffic, and the roadway belongs to vendor tents, funnel-cake
stands, food trucks, and slow Sunday crowds.

After the fair, a second stretch moves through **Midtown East**: the brownstone
**Villard Houses** (1884, McKim, Mead & White) framing the courtyard of the
Lotte New York Palace on Madison Avenue, then over to **Fifth Avenue**, where
**St. Patrick's Cathedral** rises opposite **Rockefeller Center**, flanked at
street level by the LEGO flagship and Cartier's 1905 mansion.

The soundtrack is the street itself — crowds, traffic, vendors — with light
jazz and blues instrumentals kept far beneath the city noise, and louder
private conversations removed for privacy.
