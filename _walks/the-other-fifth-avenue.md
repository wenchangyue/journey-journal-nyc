## The journey

Fifth Avenue is two streets wearing one name. The famous half ends near
Central Park's southeast corner — Apple's glass cube, Tiffany, Saks,
St. Patrick's. This walk begins where the tourists turn back: **East 100th
Street**, on upper **Museum Mile**, with Central Park's wall and trees on
the left and the **Museum of the City of New York** and **El Museo del
Barrio** passing on the right.

At 110th Street the park ends at **Duke Ellington Circle**, where Robert
Graham's 1997 bronze memorial lifts Ellington and his grand piano above
the plaza and **The Africa Center** faces the evening traffic. North of
the circle, Fifth Avenue belongs to Harlem — tenements, housing blocks,
bodegas, a green bike lane running up the middle of a quiet roadway.

Then the avenue simply stops. **Marcus Garvey Park** — Mount Morris Park
until 1973 — blocks it from 120th to 124th Street, so the walk crosses
through: mural-lined gateposts at 120th, the rocky schist outcrop, stone
steps up the Acropolis below the 1856 cast-iron **Harlem Fire Watchtower**
(the last of the city's eleven), the pool and amphitheater, and out the
north side as the sun sets behind the brownstones of the **Mount Morris
Park Historic District**.

Fifth Avenue resumes at **125th Street**, Harlem's main street, in full
blue-hour neon. The final mile drifts past stoops with lit doorway lamps,
shuttered storefronts, and apartment towers to **West 135th Street**,
where the walk — and the light — runs out.
