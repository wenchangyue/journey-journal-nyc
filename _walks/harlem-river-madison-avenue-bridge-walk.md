## The journey

This walk starts at the **Third Avenue–138th Street** subway entrance in
**Mott Haven**, in the southwestern Bronx. The station, on the IRT Pelham Line,
opened on August 1, 1918 and is served by the 6 train. Mott Haven is named for
Jordan Lawrence Mott, who bought the land for his iron works in 1849; it and
neighboring Port Morris were the first neighborhoods to be called the "South
Bronx." The camera follows **Third Avenue** south beneath the elevated roadway
to **East 135th Street**, where a sign points to the Third Avenue Bridge and
Manhattan, then turns west past newer residential towers.

At **Park Avenue** the route meets the Metro-North viaduct and the twin lift
towers of the **Harlem River Lift Bridge**, better known as the Park Avenue
Bridge. The present vertical-lift bridge opened in 1956, replacing an 1897 swing
bridge on the line first built by the New York and Harlem Railroad in 1841. It
carries four tracks used by Metro-North's Hudson, Harlem, and New Haven lines
and can open and close in about four minutes for river traffic. The walk turns
north to **East 138th Street**, passes under the railroad and the Major Deegan
Expressway, and continues west past truck yards toward the river.

## The crossing

The **Madison Avenue Bridge** is a swing bridge that carries East 138th Street
across the Harlem River between the Bronx and Manhattan. The current bridge
opened on July 18, 1910, doubling the capacity of an earlier swing bridge built
in 1884. It is 1,892 feet long with a 300-foot main span, is owned by the City
of New York, and is maintained and operated by the New York City Department of
Transportation. The video crosses on the sidewalk between the stone gatehouses
at either end, with the river below and the Manhattan skyline ahead. The
**Harlem River** itself is an 8-mile tidal strait that separates Manhattan from
the Bronx and links the Hudson River with the East River. One crossing
downstream, the **Third Avenue Bridge** is also a swing bridge; its rebuilt
structure reopened to traffic in December 2004.

## Harlem

On the Manhattan side the walk descends the East 138th Street approach and turns
south on **Fifth Avenue** past **Riverton Houses**, often called Riverton
Square. The Metropolitan Life Insurance Company proposed the project in 1944 on
a 12-acre site between 135th and 138th Streets, from Fifth Avenue to the Harlem
River; it consists of seven 13-story buildings. The route ends at Fifth Avenue
and **East 135th Street** beside P.S. 197 John B. Russwurm, an elementary
school at 2230 Fifth Avenue, where street signs point toward Harlem Hospital
Center and City College. Harlem Hospital opened on April 18, 1897 and is a
member of NYC Health + Hospitals.

## Light and sound

The walk was recorded on September 11, 2026 between about 6:34 and 7:00 PM,
under a clear sky in the golden hour before sunset. The low sun lights the
bridge steel, the water, and the Harlem brick from the side. The soundtrack is
the original ambience: traffic under the elevated highway, trains on the
viaduct, footsteps on the bridge sidewalk, and wind over the river. There is no
music, commentary, or dialogue captioning.
