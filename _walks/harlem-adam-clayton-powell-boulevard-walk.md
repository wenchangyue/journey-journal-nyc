## The journey

This walk moves north through **Central Harlem** on **Adam Clayton Powell Jr.
Boulevard**, the Harlem section of Seventh Avenue. It begins near **West 111th
Street** and stays on the same avenue to **West 133rd Street**. The video keeps
the camera's native 4K HLG HDR image and the street's original sound, with no
music or commentary.

At **West 116th Street**, the route opens into a broad multi-leg intersection
beside **Graham Court**. The New York City Landmarks Preservation Commission
records that this courtyard apartment building was commissioned by William
Waldorf Astor, designed by Clinton & Russell, and built in 1899–1901 during
Harlem's major turn-of-the-century building boom.

The route then reaches **West 125th Street**, Harlem's major commercial and
civic cross street. On the corner stands the **Adam Clayton Powell Jr. State
Office Building**. New York State records that the 19-story building was
dedicated in 1974 and renamed in 1983 for Adam Clayton Powell Jr., the first
Black member of Congress elected from New York State. The building was designed
by the Black-owned firm Ifill, Johnson & Hanchard.

Farther north, **Metropolitan Baptist Church** stands at **151 West 128th
Street**. The Landmarks Preservation Commission describes a building completed
in two stages, in 1884–85 and 1889–90, combining Gothic and Romanesque Revival
forms. The final blocks continue past neighborhood shops and apartment houses
to **West 133rd Street**, where the recorded walk ends.
