## The journey

The ride starts in **Morningside Heights**, on the streets around 120th
Street where **Riverside Church** — at 392 feet, the tallest church in the
United States — and **Grant's Tomb**, the largest mausoleum in North
America, sit a block apart above the river. From there it runs south
through the **Upper West Side**, block after block of brownstones and
awnings, down to the 90s.

Then the route drops to the water. **Riverside Park** runs four miles
from 72nd to 158th Street across 253 acres; Frederick Law Olmsted drew
its initial design in the 1870s, and Calvert Vaux and Samuel Parsons
finished the work after he was let go. The path along it is the
**Hudson River Greenway** — 12.5 miles through Manhattan and the most
heavily used bikeway in the country. On a Sunday evening it belongs to
cyclists, runners and families out for the cool air.

Riding north, the ride passes the **Cherry Walk** between 100th and
125th Streets, where Yoshino cherries descended from a **1912 gift from
Japan** lean over the path; the wide grey water with the **Palisades**
rising on the New Jersey shore; and **Riverbank State Park**, 28 acres
built on the roof of a wastewater treatment plant, 69 feet above the
river. Around 145th Street the **George Washington Bridge** (1931)
comes into view upstream, and the path runs on under the viaducts to
about 150th Street.

The way home is through **Harlem** — green bike lanes, avenues opening
up, the light going flat and blue as the evening settles in.

Riverside and street ambience throughout, mixed low under a **soft
instrumental music bed**. No narration.
