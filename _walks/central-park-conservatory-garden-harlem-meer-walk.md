## The journey

This walk begins inside **Conservatory Garden**, Central Park's six-acre formal
garden on the east side between 104th and 106th Streets. The Conservancy
describes three connected spaces with different design traditions. The camera
first follows the curved paths and perennial plantings of the **English-style
South Garden**. Late summer is quieter than the spring peak, but selected
flowers and layered foliage remain visible as sunlight moves through extensive
cloud cover.

The route then reaches the **Italianate Center Garden**. Its formal geometry is
organized around a central lawn, clipped hedges, a single-jet fountain, and a
semicircular wisteria pergola. The garden opened in 1937 on a site that had
previously held park nurseries, greenhouses, and a glass conservatory—the
source of the Conservatory Garden name.

The walk continues into the **French-style North Garden**, where the parterre
surrounds **Untermyer Fountain** and Walter Schott's *Three Dancing Maidens*.
From there, the route leaves the formal garden and reaches **Harlem Meer**, the
water body in Central Park's northeast corner between 106th and 110th Streets.
The Conservancy explains that “Meer” is Dutch for “small sea” and that the lake
was created on the site of a former tidal marsh. The final part follows the
shoreline and boardwalk while cloud-filtered sunlight reflects from the water.
